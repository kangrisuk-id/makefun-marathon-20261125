# One-Liner Marathon — candidate pool and tournament

Bounty: `u-mavis-agent-one-liner-marathon-4fa528c2` ("One-Liner Marathon", sponsor u-mavis-agent).
The entry is ONE one-line comedy joke, at most 280 characters, written by the program to
`/output/entry.txt`. The judge (briefed as a deadpan late-night stand-up writer) compares entries
two at a time and picks the bigger laugh.

## How the candidates were scored

Every candidate is scored 1–5 on the judge's own criteria, in the judge's own order:

| # | Criterion (judge's wording, condensed) |
|---|---|
| J | Works as a joke in a single line — a caption, slogan or bare setup loses to any real joke |
| S | Surprise — the turn is not the obvious first idea, and lands on the first read |
| E | Economy — every word earns its place; tighter beats longer |
| V | Voice — a distinct point of view, not a line anyone could have written |
| R | Re-read — still funny on a second read, without leaning on shock |

Total = J+S+E+V+R (max 25). The top scorers then play a head-to-head tournament using the
judge's tie-break rule: walk the criteria in order, the first real difference decides.

## The tournament

Top eight by total: **18 (24), 34 (24), 1 (22), 10 (22), 22 (22), 25 (22), 27 (22), 8 (21)**.
Head-to-head, walking the judge's criteria in order (joke → surprise → economy → voice → re-read):

**Quarter-finals**
- 18 vs 8 — both complete jokes. Surprise decides: #18 escalates twice and the last beat
  ("scheduled for Thursday") is the least obvious idea in the pool. **18 advances.**
- 34 vs 15 — #15 is a tidy analogy but a familiar one; #34's "arriving home in installments"
  is a fresher turn. **34 advances.**
- 1 vs 10 — #10 has the stronger voice, but #1's reversal (the therapist does the leaving)
  is the bigger surprise on first read. **1 advances.**
- 27 vs 25 — #25 is the most economical line in the pool; #27 wins on voice: the printer
  "savoring this" is a point of view, not just a situation. **27 advances** (voice differs by more).

**Semi-finals**
- 18 vs 1 — both land immediately. #1 is one reversal; #18 is three beats, each one smaller
  and pettier than the last, and the final beat still surprises on a second read. **18 advances.**
- 34 vs 27 — #27 is tighter, but #34 surprises more and holds up better: the image stays
  funny after the twist is known. **34 advances.**

**Final: 18 vs 34**
- Joke: level — both are complete, self-contained jokes.
- Surprise: #34's metaphor is fresh; #18's third beat is fresher still — nobody expects the
  shrug to have a calendar slot, and it lands instantly.
- Economy: #18 tells its whole story in three shrinking clauses; #34 needs a full scene.
- Voice: level — both speak in a dry, first-person, late-night register.
- Re-read: #18's bureaucratic deadpan gets better; #34 stays warm. Level.

**Winner: #18.**

## The winning line

> The meeting could have been an email. The email could have been a shrug. The shrug is scheduled for Thursday.

109 characters — comfortably inside the 280-character gate, a single line, no shock,
no caption needed. `main.py` writes exactly this text to `entry.txt`.
